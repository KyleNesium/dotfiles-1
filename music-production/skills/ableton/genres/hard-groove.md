# Hard Groove

## Genre Overview

Hard groove (sometimes called "tribal hard groove" or just "groove techno") is a techno subgenre that emerged in the late 1990s/early 2000s through Spanish and South American producers — Ben Sims, Adam Beyer, Marco Carola in his harder moods, and later Spanish acts like Hector Couto. The genre experienced a major revival around 2022-2024 as a counter-reaction to the dominance of melodic techno and minimal — Wade, Indira Paganotto, Mau P, and Marco Faraone have led the modern wave. Defined by **fast tempos (135-145 BPM)**, dense percussion (tribal drums, bongos, congas layered over driving kicks), tough acid-flavored basslines, and a relentless physical-dance-floor energy. The aesthetic is unapologetically peak-time, raw, and underground — designed for the 3am-6am part of the night when subtlety doesn't matter and physicality does.

## Characteristics

- **Instrumentation**: Hard, driving kick (often tribal-fluttered with a snap layer), bongos and congas at the fore, shaker patterns, tribal drum loops, acid bass (303 emulation), occasional vocal samples (often aggressive/diva/spoken), sparse synth elements. Less melodic than any other techno subgenre.
- **Percussion**: This is the spine. Latin tribal patterns — congas in 8th-note triplets, bongos as fills, shakers as constant texture. The genre's roots in Latin tribal house are visible everywhere.
- **Vocals**: Sparse and punchy. Pitched vocal stabs ("hey!" "yeah!" "uh!"). Diva-style spoken word from old house. Sometimes processed beyond recognition. Never full songs.
- **Production**: Loud, raw, in-your-face. Mids are pushed forward (where the percussion lives). Less reverb than progressive — clarity matters. Aggressive sidechain. Often slightly distorted — analog warmth via saturation.
- **Energy/Mood**: Aggressive, physical, peak-time. Designed for sustained dancing at maximum intensity. "Rave energy" — raw, communal, hedonistic. Less emotional, more functional.
- **Structure**: Shorter tracks than progressive (5-7 min). Builds via percussion intensification. Drops are bassline events, not melodic. Less "breakdown" structure — more continuous drive.
- **Tempo**: **135-145 BPM**. 140 is the festival sweet spot. Modern revival often at 138-142.

## Production DNA — translate to decisions

- Tempo: **140 BPM** standard. 138 for slightly slower, 142 for harder
- Key: Minor or atonal. Many tracks are essentially rhythm with bass drone — key matters less than groove
- Kick: Hard, tight, present. Layered with a "flutter" for tribal feel. Drumcode/Adam Beyer kicks are reference
- Sub: Tonal but punchy. Movement on offbeats
- Bass: Acid line. Use Live's Operator or Serum 2 with square wave + filter modulation. Drive it through Saturn 2 for grit
- Percussion: 5-8 layered tribal loops. From user's library: **Soltan Mirage**, **GBR Percussion of India**, **Cr2 House & Techno Drum Loops**, **SHARP Tech House Creatures**, **Trance and Psy Bundle** for harder textures
- Vocal: Stabs only. Use **DABRO Tekhaus Vocals** or stab samples from any of the user's tech house packs
- FX: Sirens, reverse impacts, distorted noise sweeps
- Mix: Mids forward, top end bright, bass present but not overwhelming. Drive is the goal
- Master: -7 to -5 LUFS — hard groove is loud. Modern revival tracks slam the limiter
- Genre vs user's spine: Hard groove is FAR from the user's set DNA (which is deep + emotional). This genre is a creative outlet/contrast option — when user wants to "go hard" or work on something completely different from prog
