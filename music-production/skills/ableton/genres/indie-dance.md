# Indie Dance

## Genre Overview

Indie dance (sometimes called "nu disco" in its 2010s revival form, or "dark disco" in its darker variant) is the genre where electronic dance music meets indie rock songwriting and 80s/90s pop aesthetics. It emerged in the late 2000s/early 2010s as labels like DFA, Permanent Vacation, Eskimo Recordings, and later Kompakt developed a sound bridging disco, post-punk, new wave, italo disco, and house. Defined by analog warmth, prominent live or live-sounding instruments (bass guitar, real or sampled), vocal-led songwriting, and tempos slightly below mainstream house. Hot Since 82's "Knee Deep In Sound" label and Crosstown Rebels carry the modern torch. Artists like Tiga, Eli & Fur, Hot Since 82, Jamie Jones (in his indie moods), and Damian Lazarus define the contemporary sound.

## Characteristics

- **Instrumentation**: Analog synth bass (often live-played or modeled — Moog, Juno, Prophet textures), 4-on-floor kick, electronic claps, hi-hats and shakers, vocal-led tracks (real singing, not chops), occasional live instruments (guitar, sax, percussion), warm analog pads, arpeggiated sequences.
- **Vocals**: Front-and-center. Real singing — verse-chorus structures more common than in other dance genres. Both male and female lead vocalists. Lyrics tend toward emotional/relational/nostalgic. Often pitched for "indie" feel rather than "dance diva" power.
- **Production**: Analog warmth is the signature. Tape saturation, soft compression, vinyl-style hiss. Less aggressive sidechain than techno/prog. More dynamic range — quiet verses, bigger choruses. Long reverbs but more "live room" than "cathedral."
- **Energy/Mood**: Cool, nostalgic, slightly melancholic. Late-night club + warmup territory. Suits both DJ sets and home listening. Aesthetic is "stylish underground" rather than "rave."
- **Structure**: Verse-chorus structures common (unusual for dance music). 4-7 minute tracks. Intros shorter than progressive house. Builds are restrained.
- **Tempo**: **110-122 BPM**. Slower than mainstream house. Many tracks at 115-118. Italo-disco revival tracks at 120-124.

## Production DNA — translate to decisions

- Tempo: **118 BPM** for the laid-back lane, **122 BPM** for the energetic
- Key: Major or minor — both work. The vocal melody drives the key, not the genre
- Kick: Warmer, not as dry as techno. Closer to deep-house kick
- Bass: Often a real bass guitar or modeled synth bass. Less aggressive sub presence than progressive
- Vocal: PRIMARY element. Real singing in 4-8 bar phrases. If user is producing this style, vocals must be either recorded or sourced as full performances, not chops
- Pads: Warm analog — Drift or Analog with chorus. Less wash than progressive
- Drums: Live-feeling. Add tape saturation (Saturn 2 with low drive). Hi-hats sit forward
- Master: -10 to -8 LUFS, dynamic range preserved (don't slam the limiter)
- Genre adjacent: When user wants "more pop" / "with full vocals" / "warmer & more analog", lean here from progressive-house
