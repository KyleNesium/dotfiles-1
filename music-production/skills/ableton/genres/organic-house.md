# Organic House

## Genre Overview

Organic house (sometimes called "organica" or "ethno-electronic") is a downtempo-meets-deep-house hybrid that emerged in the mid-2010s, defined by the All Day I Dream label (founded by Lee Burridge in 2011). The genre prioritizes natural, hand-played-feeling instrumentation — acoustic guitars, ethnic percussion, flutes, didgeridoos, world-music samples — over a deep, restrained electronic foundation. Tempo sits below mainstream house at 110-118 BPM, giving the genre a sunset-to-late-evening pace. Aesthetic and spiritual connections to Burning Man, Tulum, and Mediterranean sunset events. Lee Burridge, Yokoo, Bedouin, Be Svendsen, Jun Satoyama, Acid Pauli, and Sebastián Mullaert (Wa Wu We) are core. Closely adjacent to "desert tech" but with less electronic drive and more acoustic foundation. The genre often soundtracks ceremonial/spiritual events, sunset gatherings, and "non-club club" experiences.

## Characteristics

- **Instrumentation**: Acoustic guitar (often nylon-string, slide guitar, or processed steel-string), ethnic flutes (Native American, Andean pan flute, Indian bansuri), hand percussion (djembe, conga, bongos, shakers, kalimba, hang drum), ney, didgeridoo, vocal chants, light synth pads, deep sub bass. Often blends real instrumental recordings with electronic elements.
- **Vocals**: Ethereal, often wordless. Throat-singing, vocal chants in various languages (Spanish, Portuguese, Native American invocations, Hebrew). Whispered passages. Female voices common. Lyrics are spiritual/poetic rather than narrative.
- **Production**: Spacious, warm, very dynamic (less compressed than mainstream dance). Long reverb tails. Restrained sidechain. Mid-scoop EQ to let acoustic instruments breathe. Stereo width via natural ambient recordings, not artificial widening.
- **Energy/Mood**: Meditative, sunset/sunrise, transcendent. Designed for sustained presence rather than peak moments. Hypnotic through repetition with subtle micro-variations. "Healing" is the genre's claimed mood.
- **Structure**: 7-10 minute tracks. Very long intros (64+ bars). Builds happen through layering, not energy increases. No "drops" in the EDM sense — instead, peak moments are when more acoustic layers enter or a vocal chant lands.
- **Tempo**: **110-118 BPM** typical. Slower than house, faster than most downtempo. Steady four-on-the-floor or occasionally broken-beat patterns.

## Production DNA — translate to decisions

- Tempo: **115 BPM** is the sweet spot. 112 for chillier, 118 for slightly more drive
- Key: Modal scales common — Phrygian, Dorian, Mixolydian. Major keys also work. Less restrictive than melodic techno
- Kick: Soft and warm. Not punchy. Often layered with bass drum loop from world percussion
- Bass: Deep tonal sub. Less aggressive than house — sits underneath rather than driving
- Percussion: This is the genre's signature. Layer 4-8 percussion loops — hand drums, shakers, kalimba, frame drums. From user's library: **Soltan Mirage**, **Sonuscore Ethnic Vocal Phrases**, **GBR Percussion of India**, **Prime Loops SARASVATI** for sitar textures
- Lead instrument: Real acoustic guitar (record live, or sample), flute, kalimba. Less "synth" than other genres
- Pads: Subtle, warm. Long sustain. Sometimes processed acoustic instrument samples instead of synth pads
- Vocal: Wordless chants, throat singing, ethnic vocal samples. Use **W.A. Ethnic EDM Vocals** for chops, **Sonuscore Ethnic Vocal Phrases** for full phrases
- Master: -12 to -14 LUFS — much more dynamic than other genres. Preserve the breathing room
- Don't: Add heavy synth leads, big drops, aggressive compression. The genre IS its restraint
