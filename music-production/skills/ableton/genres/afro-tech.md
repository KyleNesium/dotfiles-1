# Afro Tech

## Genre Overview

Afro tech emerged in the late 2010s as a harder, more driving evolution of afro house, fusing South African afro house's polyrhythmic percussion language with techno's relentless four-on-the-floor energy and darker atmospheric palette. Where afro house lives in warm, spiritual territory, afro tech pushes toward festival main-stages and peak-time intensity — heavier kicks, more aggressive builds, less melodic sweetness. The sound was codified by South African producers like Themba and Shimza who wanted to bridge their roots in afro house with the global techno circuit. Today afro tech sits at the intersection where European labels (Keinemusik, Crosstown Rebels) meet African producers (Themba, &ME, Adam Port reinterpreting tribal rhythms), creating a global production style centered on percussive intensity and dark melodic atmospheres.

## Characteristics

- **Instrumentation**: Hard four-on-the-floor kick (tighter and punchier than afro house), layered African percussion (djembe, congas, shakers, log drums) processed with techno-grade compression; deep tonal sub bass; arpeggiated synth sequences; dark atmospheric pads; vocal samples (often pitched/chopped); minimal melodic content — drive over melody.
- **Production**: Loud and forward, designed for large sound systems. Heavy sidechain pumping. Long tribal percussion builds that intensify over 32-64 bars. Less harmonic complexity than afro house — focus is rhythmic. Mid-scooped mix to let percussion punch through. Reverb sends create depth without softening attack.
- **Energy/Mood**: Dark, driving, hypnotic. Peak-time festival energy. Less spiritual than afro house, more physical. Suited to 1am-4am main-stage sets.
- **Structure**: 6-10 minute extended tracks. Long intros (64+ bars) for DJ blending. Builds happen through percussion layering and filter automation rather than melodic development. Breakdowns strip to atmospheric pad + isolated percussion before kick returns.
- **Tempo**: **122-126 BPM** typical. Slightly faster than afro house. Steady four-on-the-floor with polyrhythmic percussion on top.

## Reference Tracks

- **Keinemusik (&ME, Rampa, Adam Port) — "Muyé"** — The defining afro-tech anthem. Tribal vocal chants over driving kick, builds endlessly through percussion layering. Festival standard.
- **&ME, Rampa, Adam Port — "Up & Down"** — Dark, hypnotic, peak-time. Keinemusik formula at its most concentrated.
- **Black Coffee, &ME, Keinemusik — "The Rapture Pt. III"** — Massive collaboration showing afro-house DNA pushed into techno space.
- **Themba — "Modern Africa"** — Title track from his album. Hard, polyrhythmic, dark melodic atmosphere.
- **Caiiro — "Drums of Africa"** — Pure tribal afro-tech. Builds through percussion alone.
- **Adam Port, Keinemusik — "Your Voice"** — Vocal-led but with afro-tech drive underneath.
- **&ME — "Woods"** — Atmospheric, dark, gradually building. The Keinemusik long-form approach.

## Production DNA — translate to decisions

- Tempo: **124 BPM** is the sweet spot
- Key: Minor (A minor, F minor, G minor common). Modal, not chord-progression-heavy
- Kick: Tight, dry, punchy — closer to techno than afro house. Layered with a sub for fundamental
- Percussion stack: 5-8 layered loops (shakers, congas, log drums, djembe, additional FX perc) — the genre IS the percussion
- Bass: Tonal sub on the root, occasional 8th-note bass stab. Sidechained heavily
- Pads: Dark, evolving, slow LFO movement. Often dissonant or modal
- Vocal: Chants, "ah" "uh" calls, pitched samples — never full songs
- FX: Reverse cymbals, white noise builds, tribal drum fills as transitions
- Master: -8 to -10 LUFS for club, -6 to -8 for DJ tools
