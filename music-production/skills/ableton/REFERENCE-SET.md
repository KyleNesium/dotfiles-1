## Production DNA — translate references to decisions

When making music "in this style", default to these patterns:

### Tempo & key
- **122 BPM** is the sweet spot. 120-124 range. Set this in every new prog session.
- Minor keys dominate — **A minor, F minor, D minor, C minor** most common. Often **modal** (Aeolian, Dorian, Phrygian for the darker ones).
- 4/4 time signature universal.

### Arrangement
- **Long format** (6-9 minutes) — these are DJ tools, not radio edits.
- **Slow build** — first 64-96 bars are drums + bass + percussion atmospheres. Vocal/melody hook drops bar 65 or 97.
- **Breakdowns at 2:30 and 5:00** — pad-and-vocal sections that strip out drums.
- **i-VI-III-VII** is the canonical progression (Am-F-C-G in A minor). Other common: i-VII-VI-VII, i-iv-VI-V.

### Sound design
- **Bass:** Rolling 16th-note or 8th-note arps on the chord root. Always heavily sidechained to the kick. Sub layer underneath.
- **Drums:** Tight dry kick (not boomy). Crisp 8th-note hats. Off-beat open hats (the "and" of 2 and 4). Lots of percussion loops, especially **modular-sounding chopped/gated stuff** (Rhythmizer territory).
- **Pads:** Long sustained analog-warm chords. Movement via slow LFO/filter sweeps. Diva / Drift / Analog territory.
- **Pluck/Lead:** Modular-sounding plucks. Heavy delay (1/4 dotted, Timeless 3 / Valhalla Delay). Reverb (VintageVerb plate).
- **Vocal chops/Phrases:** Short, atmospheric, often pitched. Reversed hits, granular textures.
- **FX layer:** White noise risers, reverse cymbals, vinyl crackle, granular textures.

### Mix
- **Wide stereo image** — Polyverse Wider on pads/atmos.
- **Controlled low-end** — sub-only on the SUB track, bass keeps fundamentals around 80-120 Hz.
- **Mid-scooped to leave room** for vocals/leads in the 1-3 kHz range.
- **Heavy sidechain pumping** on bass + pads from the kick.
- **Long reverb sends** (4-8s plate) on melodic elements.

### Master chain
- Glue compressor (2:1, 30ms attack, 100ms release, 1-2dB GR)
- Subtle saturation (Saturn 2, low drive)
- Limiter ceiling -0.3 to -1.0 dBTP, 6dB max GR for DJ-friendly pre-master

## Structural canon — the "drop is a melodic revelation"

The defining structural fact of progressive house: **the drop is a melodic revelation, not a bass impact**. EDM/big-room aesthetics drop into bass weight; prog drops into a *melodic moment* the listener has been waiting for over a long build.

- **32-bar phrases** are the rhythmic unit. Builds happen across 32 or 64 bars, not 8 or 16.
- **Intros and outros 64+ bars** — designed for DJ blending.
- **Breakdowns strip to atmospheric elements** before rebuilding. Often pad + vocal + filtered bass for 32-64 bars before the kick returns.
- **The emotional arc within each track mirrors the arc of a full DJ set** — tension, release, anticipation, catharsis.

## Reference canon (beyond the set) — listen if drifting

When unsure what "the genre" sounds like at its peak, these 6 tracks are the canonical anchors:

- **Sasha — *Xpander*** (1999) — three-part hypnotic touchstone. The classic-prog architecture in its most distilled form.
- **deadmau5 — *Strobe*** (2009) — 10 min, patient ambient piano intro → layered synths → full melodic climax. The modern emotional-arc template.
- **Eric Prydz — *Opus*** (2016) — withholds main melody for 5+ min before catharsis. The patient-build canon piece.
- **Eric Prydz — *Pjanoo*** (2008) — simple piano riff, minimal groove → euphoric peak. Proves prog can be accessible without compromise.
- **Guy J — *Lamur*** — masterclass in deep-prog restraint. Emotional impact through subtraction, not addition. Most user-set-DNA aligned.
- **Lane 8 — *Rise*** — warm organic Anjuna-prog. Reference for when the user pulls in vocal-led colour.
